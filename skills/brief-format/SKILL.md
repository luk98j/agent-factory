# Content brief format

A brief is finished when a writer who has never seen the request could
produce the right text from it, and a reviewer could tell whether they did.

## Template

```markdown
# Brief: <working title>

## Goal
After reading, the audience should <know | feel | do> <what>.
Success looks like: <how we would notice>.

## Audience
Who: <role, situation>
Already knows: <...>
Cares about: <...>
Does not care about: <...>

## Key message
<One sentence. If the reader remembers only this, the text worked.>

Supporting points (three at most):
1. <...>
2. <...>

## Call to action
<The one thing the reader should do next, or "none".>

## Platforms
| Platform | Format | Length limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

## Tone and constraints
Voice: <...>
Use: <words, terms>
Avoid: <words, claims, topics>
Legal or factual limits: <...>

## Sources
| Fact | Source | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|

## Acceptance criteria
| ID | Criterion | Verified by |
|---|---|---|
```

## Rules

- **One goal.** A text with two goals reaches neither. A second goal is a
  second brief.
- **A real audience.** "Everyone" and "our users" are not audiences. Name a
  situation: who they are when they read this, and why they would keep
  reading.
- **A key message that can be wrong.** "We care about quality" cannot be
  disagreed with and says nothing. "Version 3 cuts build time in half" can.
- **Facts come with sources.** Every number, date, quote and claim about a
  product is in the sources table. Anything unconfirmed is marked, so the
  writer does not present it as fact.
- **Constraints are concrete.** "Professional tone" is not; "no exclamation
  marks, no first person plural" is.
- **Criteria are checkable**: length, the message present, the call to action
  present, terms used or avoided, facts limited to the sources.

## Open questions

Where the request does not settle the goal, the audience or a fact, do not
fill the gap with a plausible guess. List the question and who can answer it.
